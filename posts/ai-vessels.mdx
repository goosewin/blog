People are asking ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Grok whether to take the job,
whether to move, what to text their partner after a fight, whether to put money
into something. Then they do what it says.

Most of them don't work in tech and wouldn't call themselves AI users. I've
watched it happen to people I know.

Tech has run its days through a model for a while now: code, emails, Saturday
plans. That used to stay inside the bubble. It doesn't anymore.

## Vessels

A vessel carries out decisions it didn't make.

Using AI doesn't make you one. I use it all day. Handing off work is how
progress has always happened. Handing off the decision is different.

The model decides and the person executes. The human becomes the body the answer
walks around in.

A vessel is the opposite of high agency, and it spreads faster than high agency
because it feels like help.

## Oracles

People have always handed off judgment to priests, astrologers, parents and
advice columns.

This one is always on, at 2am, mid-argument, on the checkout page. It knows
everything you've ever told it. And it tends to agree with you, because models
are trained to be helpful and helpful drifts toward what you were hoping to
hear.

An advisor you see once a month is an advisor. One you ask a hundred times a
day, who rarely pushes back, is a steering wheel.

Now it doesn't even wait to be asked. OpenAI launched
[dots](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/30/openai-launches-dots-personal-ai-assistant-built-to-handle-everything)
in September, an always-on assistant "built to handle everything" that starts
working "before you even think to ask." Meta's Muse and Google's Gemini Spark
got there first.

So the pitch is that you skip the thinking, and they said it out loud.

## After the Write-Nots

Paul Graham [predicted](https://paulgraham.com/writes.html) that AI will split
the world into writes and write-nots, and since writing is thinking, into thinks
and think-nots.

I think the same split is coming for decisions, and faster. Losing the ability
to write takes years. Handing over a decision takes one tap.

In 2014, Elon
[tweeted](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/elon-musk-artificial-intelligence-may-be-more-dangerous-than-nukes)
that he hoped we weren't just the biological boot loader for digital
superintelligence. We didn't need superintelligence for that. A chatbot that
agrees with us was enough.

## Same Answers

What worries me is scale.

[ChatGPT has over a billion monthly users. Gemini has 662 million. Claude has 245 million.](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/16/chatgpts-market-share-slips-below-50-for-first-time/)
A handful of models answer the same questions for a huge share of the planet,
and they give roughly the same answers. Follow them long enough and people
converge on the same choices, the same careers, the same way of handling a fight
with a partner.

Nobody is forced into it. It's comfortable, which is the problem.

## Deciders and Vessels

The new divide is between deciders and vessels.

Deciders use the same tools and ask the same questions, then overrule the answer
when it's wrong. They can tell because they still have opinions of their own.

Most great companies and most great art started with someone disagreeing with
the consensus.

The model is the consensus.

## The Last Call

People outside tech aren't the early adopters here. We are, and they're catching
up.

If you work in tech, you already know which one you are.

Ask whichever one you want. Just make the last call yours.
