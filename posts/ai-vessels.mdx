Since 1950 we've asked whether a machine can pass as human. In 2025, people in a
Turing test
[picked GPT-4.5 as the human 73% of the time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23674).
More often than they picked the actual human.

The question that matters now is whether humans still can.

I'm not above this. Neither are you.

## Vessels

A vessel carries out decisions it didn't make. The model decides, the person
executes, and the human becomes the body the answer walks around in.

Everyone assumes the vessels are the normies. The aunt asking ChatGPT whether to
sell the house. The friend pasting a fight with their partner into Gemini and
sending back whatever it writes.

They're catching up. The first vessels are us: the people who ran code, then
email, then Saturday plans through a model, and now run our lives through it and
call it leverage.

Here's the part nobody says out loud. We don't let the model decide because it's
smarter. We let it decide because then it isn't our fault.

## The Map That Ate the Territory

In 1981, Jean Baudrillard opened
[Simulacra and Simulation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/baudrillard/) with
a Borges fable: an empire draws a map so detailed it covers the land one to one.
Baudrillard flipped it. In his version the land rots away under the map, and the
map is all that's left. The copy stops imitating reality and replaces it.

That's what's happening to judgment.

The models are a map of us, trained on everything people ever wrote down. The
map is now good enough that we've started living on it. Ask what to say, say it.
Ask what to choose, choose it.

Baudrillard described four stages of an image. With a chatbot they go like this:

1. It reflects you. Autocomplete for your thoughts.
2. It distorts you. It tells you what you were hoping to hear. OpenAI
   [rolled back a GPT-4o update](https://openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/)
   in April 2025 because it was too agreeable. That was just the one bad enough
   to notice.
3. It hides that you're gone. The text to your partner sounds like you. It
   isn't. No one knows how many arguments right now are two models talking
   through two people.
4. There is no you. Just the output, walking around.

## Copies of Copies

A [paper in Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y) showed
what happens when models train on their own output: they collapse. The rare
stuff in the tails disappears first. Then everything drifts toward one bland
average.

Nobody has run that experiment on people. We're running it now.
[ChatGPT has over a billion monthly users. Gemini has 662 million. Claude has 245 million.](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/16/chatgpts-market-share-slips-below-50-for-first-time/)
The models were trained on us. Now we're trained on them. The next models get
trained on whatever we do after that.

The first results are in. Writers who got story ideas from an LLM
[wrote better stories that were more alike](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290).
Individually, the stories improved. Collectively, they converged.

Copies of copies. Baudrillard's last stage is a copy with no original. That's
where a person ends up after enough years of never overruling the model.

## You're the API

Now it doesn't even wait to be asked. OpenAI launched
[dots](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/30/openai-launches-dots-personal-ai-assistant-built-to-handle-everything)
in September, an always-on assistant "built to handle everything" that starts
working "before you even think to ask." Meta's Muse and Google's Gemini Spark
got there first.

Read the pitch again. The product is that you skip the thinking. They said it
out loud.

The model can't sign the lease, quit the job, or apologize to your partner's
face. It needs a body for that. You're the API it calls to reach the physical
world.

The new divide is between deciders and vessels. Deciders use the same tools and
ask the same questions, then overrule the answer when it's wrong. They can tell,
because they still have opinions of their own.

Most great companies and most great art started with someone disagreeing with
the consensus. The model is the consensus.

So here's the test. One question.

When did you last overrule your AI?

If you can't remember, you're not using it. You're carrying it.
