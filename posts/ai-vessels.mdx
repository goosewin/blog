Since 1950 the Turing test has asked whether a machine can pass as human. In
2025 one did. In a three-party test, people chatted with a human and an AI at
the same time and
[picked GPT-4.5 as the human 73% of the time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23674),
more often than they picked the actual person.

What I keep thinking about is the reverse test: whether people still pass as
human once a model is making their decisions.

People are asking ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Grok whether to take the job,
whether to move, what to text their partner after a fight, whether to put money
into something. Then they do what it says. I've watched it happen to people I
know, and I'm not above it either. The model decides and the person executes,
which makes the person a vessel: the body the answer walks around in.

## Baudrillard's map

In 1981 Jean Baudrillard opened
[Simulacra and Simulation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/baudrillard/) with
a Borges story about an empire whose cartographers draw a map as large as the
empire itself. In Borges, later generations find the map useless and leave it in
tatters in the desert. In Baudrillard's version the territory rots away and
people end up living on the map.

Language models are a map of us, built from an enormous amount of what people
have written down. The map is now detailed enough that a lot of people check it
before they check their own judgment, and then act on it.

Baudrillard described an image passing through stages. First it reflects
reality, then it distorts it, then it hides that the reality is gone, and
finally it refers to nothing at all. Chatbots already distort. OpenAI
[rolled back a GPT-4o update](https://openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/) in
April 2025 because it had become too agreeable, and that was only the version
bad enough for people to notice. An advisor you see once a month is an advisor.
One you ask a hundred times a day, who rarely pushes back, is a steering wheel.

They also hide the absence. A text a model wrote for you after a fight still
sounds like you, and there is no way to know how many arguments right now are
two models talking through two people.

## Model collapse

A [2024 paper in Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y)
showed what happens when models are trained on the output of earlier models. The
models collapse. Rare cases in the tails disappear first, and after enough
generations the output narrows toward a single average.

The same loop now runs through people.
[ChatGPT alone has over a billion monthly users](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/16/chatgpts-market-share-slips-below-50-for-first-time/).
The models learned from us, we take their answers, and the next models will
learn from whatever we write after that.

In a
[2024 study in Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290),
writers who got story ideas from an LLM wrote stories that judges rated as more
creative and better written, and those stories were more similar to each other
than stories written without help. Baudrillard's last stage is a copy with no
original, and that's where a person ends up after years of never overruling the
model.

## Grok Bot, Muse and dots

The assistants keep getting better, which makes this easier. xAI's
[Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot) is outstanding: a team of
always-on agents with their own computer that work inside your apps around the
clock. Meta's
[Muse](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/),
out since September, is great too. Using them doesn't make anyone a vessel.
Handing off work is how progress has always happened. Handing off the decision
is different.

Then there's OpenAI's
[dots](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/30/openai-launches-dots-personal-ai-assistant-built-to-handle-everything),
which launched at the end of September and is the worst personal assistant
product I've seen. OpenAI sells it as "built to handle everything" and says it
starts working "before you even think to ask." The pitch is that you skip the
thinking, and they said it out loud.

A model can't sign a lease or apologize to someone's face, so for anything in
the physical world it needs a person to carry out the answer, and a vessel is
the API it calls.

The new divide is between deciders and vessels. Deciders use the same tools and
ask the same questions, then overrule the answer when it's wrong. They can tell
when it's wrong because they still have opinions of their own.

Most great companies and most great art started with someone disagreeing with
the consensus. The model is the consensus.
